Source: Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Government of India, "Standard Treatment Guidelines: Snakebite" (June 2016), first aid section (https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/3941.pdf); NHS, "Snake bites" (https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/snake-bites/). In India the ambulance number is 108.
Key fact: First aid for a snake bite:
- Reassure the person and keep them calm: most snake bites are from non-venomous snakes, and panic makes the heart beat faster.
- Keep the bitten arm or leg as still as possible, like a broken limb. Hold it with a splint (a stick or piece of wood) and cloth, but do not block the blood supply or press hard.
- Remove rings, watches, jewellery, shoes and tight clothing near the bite, because they can act like a tight band when swelling starts.
- Take the person to the nearest health centre or hospital immediately. They must not walk, run or drive themselves there.
- Waiting for an ideal ambulance should not delay the journey: a motorbike can be used if a third person sits behind the patient to support them.
- Give them nothing to eat or drink until they reach the hospital.
Caveat: Limits of this advice:
- Do not tie a tight band, rope or tourniquet: it does not work and can cause loss of the limb.
- Do not wash, cut, suck, rub or massage the bite, and do not put herbs or chemicals on it.
- Traditional remedies, black stones and herbal medicines do not help and delay treatment.
- Do not try to catch or kill the snake. If it is safe, take a photo of it for the doctor.
- Do not take aspirin or ibuprofen, as they can make bleeding worse.
- This is first aid only. Antivenom, the treatment for a venomous bite, can only be given by a doctor at a health facility.
