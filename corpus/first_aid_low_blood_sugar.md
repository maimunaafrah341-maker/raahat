Source: NHS, "Low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia)" (https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/low-blood-sugar-hypoglycaemia/). UK sources say call 999; in India the ambulance number is 108.
Key fact: Low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia), usually in someone with diabetes:
- Signs include feeling hungry, sweating, shaking, feeling dizzy, anxious or irritable, a fast heartbeat, blurred vision and confusion.
- If they are awake and can swallow, give something that raises blood sugar quickly, such as a small glass of fruit juice or a sugary fizzy drink, or 5 glucose tablets.
- Check their blood sugar after 10 to 15 minutes. If it is still below 4mmol/L, give another sugary drink or snack and check again after 10 minutes.
- When they feel better, give them something more filling, such as biscuits or a sandwich.
- If they are unconscious, put them in the recovery position and give a glucagon injection straight away if one is available.
- Call 108 if they are unconscious or not responding normally and no glucagon is available, or if they have not recovered within 10 minutes of a glucagon injection.
Caveat: Limits of this advice:
- Do not give food or drink to someone who is unconscious: they cannot swallow safely.
- This is first aid only. It does not replace emergency medical care.
