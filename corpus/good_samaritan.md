Source: Section 134A, Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 (inserted by the Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act,
2019), and the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways guidelines for the protection of Good
Samaritans (notification of 12 May 2015), approved and made binding by the Supreme Court in SaveLife
Foundation & Anr. v. Union of India & Anr. (2016) (https://www.livelaw.in/road-accidents-read-sc-approved-guidelines-protect-good-samaritans).
Key fact: A bystander or "Good Samaritan" who helps a road accident victim or brings them to hospital
is not liable for any civil or criminal action for that help. Under the guidelines, they should be
allowed to leave the hospital straight away; the hospital must not detain them or ask them to pay
for registration or admission (unless they are a family member or relative of the injured person);
giving their name and contact details is voluntary; and if they ask, the hospital must give them an
acknowledgement that they brought the injured person in. Hospitals are to display a charter saying
this, in Hindi, English and the local language.
Caveat: Limits of this advice: these protections are for someone who helps an accident victim who is
not their relative; a family member can still be asked about payment. They concern the helper, not
what treatment the victim will get or whether it is free.
Use this when the person says they brought a stranger or an accident victim to hospital, or are
worried about getting into trouble, being asked to pay, or being made to stay for the police.
