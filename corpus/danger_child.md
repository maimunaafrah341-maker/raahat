Source: NHS, "Is your baby or toddler seriously ill?" (https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/health/is-your-baby-or-toddler-seriously-ill/). UK sources say call 999; in India the ambulance number is 108.
Key fact: Call 108 or go to a hospital emergency department straight away if a baby or young child:
- Has swollen lips, tongue or throat, with difficulty swallowing or talking.
- Is breathing very fast or having difficulty breathing - they may make grunting noises or suck in their tummy when they breathe.
- Has blue, grey, pale or blotchy skin, lips or tongue. On brown or black skin, this may be easier to see on the palms of the hands or soles of the feet.
- Has skin that feels cold or damp (clammy).
- Has a rash that does not fade when you press it.
- Has a very high temperature: 38°C or higher under 3 months old, or 39°C or higher at 3 to 6 months old.
- Has a very low temperature (below 36°C), or feels cold to touch or is shivery.
- Has a weak, high-pitched or continuous cry.
- Has fainted, is difficult to wake, or cannot be woken.
- Has not had a pee in the past 12 hours.
- Is having a seizure (fit).
Caveat: Limits of this advice:
- Trust your instincts: get immediate help if your child is unwell and you think something is seriously wrong.
- This is general guidance, not a diagnosis. It does not replace a doctor or emergency care.
