Source: Cashless Treatment of Road Accident Victims Scheme, 2025 — Ministry of Road Transport and
Highways notification S.O. 2015(E), 5 May 2025, under section 162 of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988;
guidelines S.O. 2489(E), 4 June 2025; implemented by the National Health Authority with State Health
Agencies; continued as "PM RAHAT" (Road Accident Victim Hospitalisation and Assured Treatment),
announced February 2026 (https://www.newsonair.gov.in/government-announces-launch-of-pm-rahat-scheme-for-cashless-treatment-of-up-to-rs-1-lakh-50-thousand-for-road-accident-victims).
Key fact: Any person injured in a road accident caused by a motor vehicle, on any road in India, may
be able to get cashless treatment of up to ₹1.5 lakh per victim, for up to 7 days from the date of the
accident, at a designated hospital. Hospitals are paid by the government from the Motor Vehicle
Accident Fund, not by the victim. Hospitals empanelled under Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY can be among the
designated hospitals.
Caveat: Limits of this advice: this only applies to road accidents involving a motor vehicle; it covers
up to ₹1.5 lakh and only the first 7 days; at a hospital that is not designated, only treatment to
stabilise the patient is covered under the scheme. The accident usually has to be reported to the
police for the claim. Always tell them to ask the hospital's desk whether it is a designated hospital
for the road accident cashless scheme.
Use this when the person was hurt in a road accident (hit by a vehicle, bike or auto accident, crash)
and is worried about the cost of treatment.
