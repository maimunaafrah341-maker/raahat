Source: NHS, "Anaphylaxis" (https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/anaphylaxis/). UK sources say call 999; in India the ambulance number is 108.
Key fact: Severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) - signs and what to do:
- Signs include swelling of the throat and tongue, difficulty breathing or breathing very fast, a tight throat or hoarse voice, wheezing, feeling faint or fainting, and blue, grey or pale skin, lips or tongue.
- Use an adrenaline auto-injector (such as an EpiPen) if the person has one.
- Call 108 and say you think it is anaphylaxis (a severe allergic reaction).
- Lie them down. You can raise their legs. If they are struggling to breathe, raise their shoulders or let them sit up slowly.
- If it was an insect sting, try to remove the sting if it is still in the skin.
- If there is no improvement after 5 minutes, use a second adrenaline auto-injector.
Caveat: Limits of this advice:
- The person should not stand or walk at any time, even if they feel better.
- This is first aid only. It does not replace emergency medical care.
