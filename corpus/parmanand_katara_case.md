Source: Pt. Parmanand Katara v. Union of India & Ors., (1989) 4 SCC 286 / AIR 1989 SC 2039 —
Supreme Court of India, 1989 (full text: https://www.globalhealthrights.org/pt-parmandand-katara-v-union-of-india-and-ors/).
Key fact: A man hit by a car in a road accident was refused treatment by the nearest hospital and
sent to another one, and died on the way. The Supreme Court held that every doctor, whether at a
government hospital or otherwise, has the professional obligation to give their services to protect
the life of an injured person, and that formalities under the Criminal Procedure Code or any other
local law (such as waiting for the police in an accident or "medico-legal" case) should not stand in
the way of treating an injured person. No law or State action can delay this duty.
Caveat: Limits of this advice: this is about treatment being started without waiting for police
formalities; it does not say the treatment will be free, and the police may still need to be informed
later. Say this as what the law expects of doctors, not as a promise about any one hospital.
Use this when the person was hurt in an accident, a fight or any case the hospital calls a "police
case" or "MLC", or when they are told to go to a government hospital or wait for the police first.
