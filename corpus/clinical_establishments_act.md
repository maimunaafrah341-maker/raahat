Source: The Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act, 2010 (Central Act No. 23 of 2010),
sections 2(o), 10, 12(2), 32 and 40 (official text: https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2010/the-clinical-establishments-(registration-and-regulation)-act,-2010.pdf).
Telangana adopted the Act by resolutions of the Legislative Assembly (25.03.2017) and Legislative Council
(27.03.2017) under Article 252(1) (Telangana Gazette, L.A. Bill No. 15 of 2025, 31 August 2025:
https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/bills_states/telangana/2025/Bill15of2025TS.pdf), and applied the
Telangana State Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Rules, 2011 by Government Order in
June 2022 (https://telanganatoday.com/telangana-govt-adopts-clinical-establishment-act-to-regulate-private-hospitals).
Key fact: Section 12(2): "The clinical establishment shall undertake to provide within the staff and
facilities available, such medical examination and treatment as may be required to stabilise the
emergency medical condition of any individual who comes or is brought to such clinical establishment."
To stabilise (section 2(o)) means treatment so that, within reasonable medical probability, the condition
will not materially get worse during a transfer to another hospital. The duty is for any individual who
comes or is brought in; the law sets no condition of paying first.
Key fact: Each district has a district registering authority, chaired by the District Collector with the
District Health Officer as convenor (section 10). It can inspect a hospital and inquire into its work, and
can cancel the registration of a hospital that does not keep the conditions of registration, which include
section 12(2) (section 32). Breaking a provision of the Act can be fined up to ₹10,000 the first time,
up to ₹50,000 the second time and up to ₹5,00,000 after that (section 40).
Caveat: Limits of this advice: this only requires STABILISATION, within the staff and facilities the
hospital has. It does not make full treatment free: a hospital can bill for care beyond stabilisation, and
can transfer a stabilised patient to another hospital. Say this as what the law requires, not as a promise
about what any one hospital will do.
Use this when the person can't pay, is asked to pay before emergency treatment, is turned away or told to
go elsewhere, or wants to complain about a hospital that refused emergency treatment.
