Source: St John Ambulance, "Severe bleeding" (https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/severe-bleeding/); British Red Cross, "Bleeding heavily" (https://www.redcross.org.uk/first-aid/learn-first-aid/bleeding-heavily); NHS inform, "Severe bleeding first aid" (https://www.nhsinform.scot/tests-and-treatments/first-aid/severe-bleeding-first-aid/). UK sources say call 999; in India the ambulance number is 108.
Key fact: First aid for a deep cut or heavy bleeding, while waiting for help:
- Call 108, or ask someone to call while you help.
- Press firmly on the wound with a clean, non-fluffy cloth or dressing, and keep pressing until help arrives.
- Secure the dressing with a bandage: firm enough to keep the pressure on, but not so tight that it stops circulation.
- Help the person lie down, and raise and support their legs above the level of their heart, to help prevent shock.
Caveat: What not to do, and limits of this advice:
- If something is stuck in the wound, do not pull it out. It may be slowing the bleeding. Press on either side of it instead.
- This is first aid only. It does not replace emergency medical care.
